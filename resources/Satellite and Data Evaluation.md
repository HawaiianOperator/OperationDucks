### Verified hourly source — NOAA GMGSI

Start the broad weather imagery layer with NOAA's Global Mosaic of Geostationary Satellite Imagery. NOAA lists five contributing spacecraft: GOES-18, GOES-19, Himawari-9, Meteosat-9 and Meteosat-10. GMGSI offers roughly 8 km imagery across 60°S–60°N. Longwave infrared works by day and night. Visible, shortwave infrared and water vapor are additional channels. The mosaic is imagery. Keep fire detections, surface temperature retrievals and rainfall as independent products. [NOAA coverage](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/products/imagery/gmgsi/) · [Public data registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-gmgsi/)

- **Access:** Public bucket `noaa-gmgsi-pds` in `us-east-1`, no AWS account required. Verified prefixes are `GMGSI_LW/`, `GMGSI_VIS/`, `GMGSI_SW/`, `GMGSI_WV/` and `GMGSI_SSR/`. Longwave files use `GMGSI_LW/YYYY/MM/DD/HH/`. List with `aws s3 ls --no-sign-request s3://noaa-gmgsi-pds/GMGSI_LW/`. [Bucket](https://noaa-gmgsi-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/index.html) · [Registry configuration](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/noaa-gmgsi.yaml)
- **Measured sample:** Anonymous S3 listing returned four consecutive November 14, 2026 UTC slots, 00:00 through 03:00. Their NetCDF objects were **7.29–7.34 MB** and S3 LastModified was **39:01–39:21 after the nominal start** or about **29 minutes after the filename's nominal scan end**. This is a four-object posting-time sample. It does not measure individual contributing pixel ages or application ingest latency. One retained longwave frame per hour works out to about **175 MB/day** at the observed size. [Example object](https://noaa-gmgsi-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/GMGSI_LW/2026/11/14/02/GLOBCOMPLIR_v3r0_blend_s202611140200000_e202611140209599_c202611140233472.nc)
- **Integration reference:** LibreWXR documents a GMGSI ingestion and tile-rendering workflow. Preserve provider timestamps and label the display as a multi-satellite mosaic. Use NOAA's published 60°S–60°N extent for coverage promises until valid-pixel bounds are tested. [LibreWXR configuration](https://github.com/JoshuaKimsey/LibreWXR/blob/main/docs/configuration-reference.md)

### Hourly operating specification

**Target:** publish one map snapshot per UTC hour and allow up to two hours of observation age per region and layer. Run collection every 5 minutes. Use the latest complete scan available at publication time. The map stays interactive between snapshots. Each cell or tile retains its source observation time, so an hourly mosaic can contain imagery acquired at different times. Scan frequency alone does not prove data delivery within two hours.

### Processing and acceptance

1. Track each source by platform, product, scan start and end, revision, native CRS, footprint and first-seen UTC. Enumerate recent object prefixes across date boundaries. Use an idempotent key for each file and verify all required bands and chunks before publishing a complete scan. Handle late files as revisions.
2. Start with one infrared band per geostationary platform. Serve tiled imagery and retain source navigation, units, calibration and quality flags in metadata. For overlapping disks, prefer valid pixels with a better viewing angle, then favor newer data within equivalent viewing conditions. Keep visible imagery daylight-only. Show gaps at the limbs and poles.
3. Publish one hourly snapshot. Mark tiles up to 60 minutes old as current, 60–120 minutes as delayed, and over 120 minutes as stale. Expose acquisition UTC and source in the map and AI answers. Keep a region with no valid sample marked unavailable.
4. Run a 72-hour measurement across the Americas, Europe, Africa, East Asia, Australia, the Indian Ocean and both polar regions. Log acquisition-to-first-seen delay, acquisition-to-publish delay, valid area, missing or partial scans, and retained bytes. Provisional pass criterion for declared core imagery regions is at least 95% of region-hours with at least 90% of the region covered by valid infrared pixels aged two hours or less. Report polar and specialist-layer results individually. This acceptance threshold is proposed and has not been measured.
5. GOES East, GOES West, Himawari and one Meteosat FCI disk give a theoretical **576 full-disk scan times per day** at uninterrupted 10-minute cadence. Hourly publication yields up to **96 platform frames per day** across those four sources, before polar passes and additional bands. Measure bytes per scan from source objects and apply a retention window before adding all bands.

Use the latest source acquisition timestamp as the dashboard clock. Scan cadence describes how often an instrument observes. Delivery latency describes how old the data is when the dashboard receives it.

## Coverage at a glance

- **Global dashboard** — Start with IMERG for precipitation, NASA POWER for daily weather and solar estimates, SMAP for soil moisture, and MODIS for historical context.
- **Local detail** — Add Landsat vegetation and ECOSTRESS surface temperature where usable scenes overlap the replay. Keep Meteomatics for wind.
- **Live reception** — Use GOES-18 for a California weather demo or XI-IV to demonstrate a CubeSat beacon reception.

### Radio formats and throughput

- **GOES HRIT/EMWIN, 1694.1 MHz** — CCSDS-framed HRIT/LRIT imagery and weather messages. The original GOES products are NetCDF-4. NOAA lists Himawari imagery on GOES West's rebroadcast. At 400 kbps, the nominal stream is about 4.3 GB per day of continuous capture before processing. [NOAA product and format list](https://ospo.noaa.gov/operations/goes/hrit/index.html)
- **GOES GRB, 1686.600 MHz** — Higher-throughput weather downlink. Dual circular polarization and 31 Mbps aggregate, approximately 335 GB per day of nominal stream capacity. NOAA specifies a nominal 4.5 m dish under its stated receiver-noise assumptions. [NOAA GRB specification](https://noaasis.noaa.gov/GOES/GRB/specifications.html)
- **NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 HRD, 7812 MHz** — Binary channel access data units carry science and calibration packets. Process the received packets into calibrated, geolocated products such as VIIRS radiance and brightness temperature. NOAA distributes native JPSS products in HDF5. A 10-minute capture at 15 or 25 Mbps is about 1.13 or 1.88 GB respectively at the nominal bit rate. [NOAA framing specification](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2022-03/JPSS-1SCHRDtoDBSRFICDRevA-470-REF-00184February9%2C2015.pdf) · [NOAA HDF5 product guide](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2022-03/474-00019-01_JPSS-API-Users-Guide-Vol-I_0123A.pdf)
- **Metop AHRPT** — Framed binary instrument packets. **XI-IV** — CW Morse beacon. Store a station observation as metadata with a waterfall or audio artifact and any decoded text. [EUMETSAT specification](https://user.eumetsat.int/s3/eup-strapi-media/TD_18_Metop_Direct_Readout_AHRPT_Technical_Description_v3_A_1cb789b653.pdf) · [SatNOGS observation log](https://network.satnogs.org/users/lw2dtz/)

These are decimal line-rate estimates, calculated as bits per second times capture duration divided by eight. Actual retained science files vary with framing, compression, reception, and product selection.

## First tests

1. Confirm the receive site and radio. Calculate a local XI-IV pass and check GOES-18 look angles.
2. Try one XI-IV pass or use a public station reception to develop the map panel. Save the NORAD ID, station, start and end UTC, frequency, and original waterfall or decoded beacon link.
3. For the live weather test, decode one GOES-18 image and record its observation time and local receive time. Keep a labeled recorded example for rehearsal.
4. For the science-layer test, pull one IMERG accumulation and one Landsat NDVI scene for the proposed replay. Verify file readability, spatial overlap, timestamps, masks, and source attribution. Check SMAP depth and quality flags, POWER parameter units and time convention, and the full MODIS composite window before comparing layers. Keep source records available to the AI guide for citations.
